How the rules showed up on this chart

  1. Support existed. The most recent confirmed pivot low was $61.79. On 2020-03-09, the low was $63.39 — inside the zone up to about $64.90 (support + 1 × ATR $3.11) — but the close finished at $64.15, back above support.
  2. Trend filter passed. That close was also above the 200-day average ($59.57), and 6-month momentum was +22.3%.
  3. Entry next open. The signal was only known after the 2020-03-09 close, so the buy happened at the 2020-03-10 open for $66.79 — not at the signal close.
  4. Defined risk first. The initial stop stayed fixed at $52.46. Price could have been wrong immediately; the strategy did not move the stop to feel safer.
  5. Target switches the exit. Once a close reached $78.86 on 2020-06-05, the stop changed from fixed to a 3 × ATR chandelier trail off the highest close — the teal dashed line.
  6. Let winners run. Instead of selling at the first target, the position rode the trail for months and exited on 2020-09-04 at $116.28 when the close fell through the trail.

Why this example is useful

  • It shows the strategy is not “buy the dip and hope.” The entry required the level to hold into the close and the trend to still be up.
  • It shows the two-phase exit: a wide fixed stop early, then a trailing stop only after the target is reached.
  • It also shows the honest caveat: this was a big winner (+73.6%), but the same rules produced losers too — the backtest win rate is about 55%, not 100%.
